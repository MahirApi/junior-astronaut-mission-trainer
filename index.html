POWER ROUTING

SET PRIORITIES

Power is a shared pool. Habitat essentials draw 4 units first; the remaining power is divided proportionally among your slider requests. If requests exceed the budget, every system gets less than requested. Unused power charges the battery. Your plan carries forward each Sol until you change it. Major repairs need a selected target, enough delivered power and one of your three spare kits.

Science & data traceability

What is real: NASA's DONKI service records observed and analyzed space-weather events. The optional lookup in this prototype retrieves real Solar Energetic Particle (SEP) records and displays their recorded date and source link.

What is simulated: The Sol 23 shelter challenge is an educational scenario. The game does not convert DONKI records into a Mars radiation dose, and the crew-safety changes are not measured NASA telemetry.

NASA data source

  • NASA Open APIs — DONKI documentation
  • NASA Community Coordinated Modeling Center — DONKI
  • NASA Life Support Baseline Values and Assumptions Document (BVAD)

Model assumptions

  • Power, battery, oxygen, water, food and crop units are teaching-game units, not engineering specifications.
  • Life-support power influences oxygen production and water recovery in a simplified way.
  • Greenhouse power and water build crop progress over several Sols; harvests are not immediate.
  • Radiation protection combines a passive shelter-upgrade bonus with power allocated to shelter procedures. No active magnetic shield is modeled.
  • Solar-array dust, multi-Sol dust storms, filter blockage, crop blight, habitat seal leaks and heater faults are simplified crisis mechanics, not a forecast for a specific Mars location or an engineering failure-rate model.
  • The campaign lasts 30 game Sols. Crisis timing and recovery rates are authored for learning about prioritization; they are not operational mission procedures.
